Bio

I’m an aspiring cloud engineer based in Los Angeles, focused on building and securing infrastructure on AWS.

I’ve always been a tech enthusiast, particularly when it comes to IT and the intersection between technology and modern-day business operations. That curiosity is what eventually pulled me toward cloud engineering. I became fascinated by tools like Lambda, EC2, S3, and DynamoDB and, more importantly, what they allow you to build. The idea that you can control infrastructure powerful enough to support companies, products, and people around the world — all from a laptop — was hard for me not to get interested in.

That curiosity turned into a real interest, and somewhere along the way, the work became genuinely fun.

What keeps me interested is how foundational cloud infrastructure is. Most people never see it, but it sits behind nearly everything they use, and that role is only getting bigger. I like learning how those systems are built, how they fail, and what it takes to keep them reliable and secure.

AI has become another part of that curiosity. I’ve watched it evolve from something that felt experimental into technology that can actually become part of the systems we build. What interests me most isn’t AI on its own, but what happens when you combine it with cloud infrastructure. Connecting services like EventBridge with Amazon Bedrock and models like Claude opens up some genuinely cool possibilities — infrastructure that can react to events, interpret what’s happening, and help automate decisions instead of simply executing a predefined set of steps.

That intersection between cloud, security, automation, and AI is something I want to explore much further.

Right now, I’m especially interested in cloud security: understanding how cloud environments are attacked, how activity can be detected, and how AWS services can work together to respond.

I’m also studying Computer Information Technology, which has given me room to explore the overlap between IT, cloud infrastructure, security, and software engineering without making algorithm-heavy application development the center of what I do.

This site is where I document that process — the projects I build, the labs I break, the certifications I earn, and everything I learn in between.
